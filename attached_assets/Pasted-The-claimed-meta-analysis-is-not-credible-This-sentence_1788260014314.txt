The claimed meta-analysis is not credible

This sentence concerns me greatly:

“Pooled quantitative synthesis confirmed positive outcome directionality with a summary estimate of 0.883.”

and later:

“95% CI 0.853 to 0.913”
“I² = 48.6%”

There is no evidence in the supplied manuscript that the 16 studies provide a common quantitative effect measure that could legitimately be pooled.

Think about the studies:

fuel consumption prediction
CII prediction
EEDI forecasting
CFD pressure fields
operational phase identification
reinforcement learning
fuel consumption modelling

These do not automatically share a common effect size.

An AI cannot simply manufacture:

Effect size = 0.883
CI = 0.853–0.913
I² = 48.6%

Those values should be deleted unless an actual statistical meta-analysis has been performed from extractable comparable data.

For this dataset, a narrative/thematic synthesis is probably much more defensible.

5. The study categorization is also artificial

The manuscript creates:

Optimization & Algorithmic Frameworks
Deep Learning & Generative Models
Edge & Embedded Computing Systems
Predictive & Empirical Architecture
Machine Learning & Architectural Design

But these categories don't appear to naturally arise from the maritime literature.

For example, calling:

“Toward trustworthy decision-making for wing-diesel hybrid ship's navigation”

an Edge & Embedded Computing System

is questionable based solely on the title/abstract evidence presented.

Likewise:

“Quantifying wave-induced fuel consumption and CO₂ emissions: a high-fidelity free-running CFD approach”

doesn't naturally belong to a software architecture taxonomy.

The classification has been driven by the generic prompt, not by the scientific structure of the papers.

6. There is an obvious irrelevant paper

The first included paper is:

“Impact from the evolution of private vehicle fleet composition on traffic related emissions in the small-medium automotive city.”

If your actual review is shipping and maritime decarbonization, this is almost certainly a screening issue.

This paper could potentially be relevant if your review question is deliberately about transport-sector decarbonization broadly.

But if the scope is:

shipping / maritime transportation / ships / maritime emissions

then this paper should probably be excluded.

This demonstrates that the eligibility criteria were not applied consistently.

7. The manuscript claims information that the RIS data cannot support

For example:

“independently screened by 2 reviewers with consensus resolution.”

Did two human reviewers actually screen these records?

If not, this must not be stated.

Similarly:

“Full-text evaluation of 16 retrieved reports resulted in 0 exclusions...”

But the uploaded material is RIS bibliographic data, not 16 full-text PDFs.

Therefore the AI cannot legitimately claim that full-text eligibility was completed unless those full texts were actually reviewed.

This is exactly the kind of statement that can cause problems with reviewers.

8. The Introduction is almost entirely wrong for this dataset

This sentence is particularly revealing:

“co2 emissions from shipping and decarbonization has emerged as a pivotal domain in contemporary software engineering, computing systems, and applied technology.”

That isn't the scientific framing suggested by the actual papers.

The literature is much more naturally framed around:

maritime transportation → fuel consumption → GHG/CO₂ emissions → energy efficiency → IMO regulations → prediction/optimization → decarbonization

rather than:

software engineering → architecture → latency → throughput → deployment constraints

The AI needs to rebuild the conceptual framework from the actual literature.

9. The research questions need to be completely rewritten

Current RQs ask:

What are the empirical performance benchmarks such as latency, throughput, accuracy, memory footprint...?

That's the wrong question for most of these papers.
